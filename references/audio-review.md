# Audio review for spoken videos

Read this when adding music, sound effects, source audio, or changing speed.

1. **Identify the priority.** Speech should remain intelligible. A quoted clip may briefly become the focus. Keep music and effects on separate tracks so their levels can change without changing narration.
2. **Choose cues for meaning.** A page turn can introduce a source; a restrained movement can mark a chapter; a click can follow a visible state change. Avoid sounding every caption or cut. Put proposed cues on the actual timeline, including their decay, and inspect their spacing.
3. **Audition in context.** Compare a representative speech-heavy stretch and a visual transition at the same dialogue level. Listen once with music alone, once with effects, then with both. Note whether an effect can be heard, masks a word onset, repeats too often, or clashes with the music. A quieter asset may need a different sound rather than more gain.
4. **Handle the music seam.** Measure the track's real length and inspect any fade or silence at the end. If it must loop, make the join smooth at a musical point. Fade the bed at the opening and ending without fading the spoken track unless requested.
5. **Check the full edit.** Measure integrated loudness and true peak, then listen continuously to the opening, recurring cues, loop seams, dense speech, source-audio handoffs, and the ending. LUFS and peak values show technical level, not perceptual balance.

After a narrow revision, preview the affected stretch and its nearby joins before rendering the entire output. Preserve the previous version and report whether the new full file has actually been listened to.
